    Method and assumptions

    How the simulator works

    A transparent scaling model: a fixed baseline city is calibrated once, and the proposed city is that baseline rescaled by activity, weather and greenery. Everything below is visible so it can be explained, questioned and extended in class.

    Objective

    Let a planner or student change the inputs of a proposed city (traffic, industry, energy, greenery, weather) and see the effect on six CPCB pollutants, the resulting AQI, carbon emissions and a composite sustainability score, against a fixed baseline.

    Calculation steps

    1. Activity. Vehicle, industry and population inputs are divided by the baseline values to get activity ratios. EV share removes exhaust emissions but not tyre, brake and road-dust particles. Industrial control efficiency and clean-energy share lower industrial output.
    2. Source apportionment. Each pollutant's baseline concentration is split into regional background, vehicles, industry and other local sources (dust, biomass, households). Each local share is multiplied by its activity ratio.
    3. Meteorology. A ventilation index (wind speed × mixing height) dilutes local contributions. Mixing height depends on temperature and season. Humidity raises particulate matter. Monsoon rain washes particles out. Temperature drives ozone.
    4. Greenery. Tree and vegetation cover removes a fraction of each pollutant by deposition, with diminishing returns as cover grows.
    5. Concentration to sub-index. Each concentration is converted with CPCB breakpoints. The AQI is the largest sub-index and its pollutant is the dominant one.
    6. Carbon and score. CO₂ is summed by sector, minus a green-cover sink. The sustainability score blends air quality, carbon per person, green cover, EV share and clean energy.

    Equations

    C(p) = w · [ bg(p) + Σs L(s,p) · A(s) · f_vent · f_green · f_hum ]
f_vent  = ( VI_ref / VI ) ^ k          VI = max(u, 0.5 m/s) × H
H       = clamp(300 + 20·T, 300, 1200) m × season factor
f_green = (1 − r(p)·G(g)) / (1 − r(p)·G(g0))      G(g) = 1 − exp(−g / 30)
Sub-index  I = (I_hi − I_lo) / (BP_hi − BP_lo) · (C − BP_lo) + I_lo
AQI = max over pollutants of I
CO₂ = ICE vehicles × 2.3 kg × congestion + EVs × 1.15 kg × (1 − clean)
      + industries × 14 t × (1 − 0.4·clean) + people × 1.0 kg × (1 − clean)
      − green sink (877 t/day at 100% cover)

    w is the monsoon washout factor, L the baseline load, A the activity ratio, r(p) the removal fraction, u wind speed, T temperature, g green cover in percent.

    Assumptions and limits

    • Coefficients are illustrative, chosen to resemble a mid-size Indian city. They are not fitted to measurements, so results show direction and relative size, not forecasts.
    • The model is a single-box, daily-average scale. It has no street-level variation, no atmospheric chemistry transport and no day-to-day carry-over.
    • Ozone is simplified: it rises with temperature and with vehicle and industrial precursors, and it is damped by dilution and greenery.
    • The AQI is computed from modelled 24-hour and 8-hour means. The official CPCB AQI needs monitored data with completeness rules.
    • The 24-hour view redistributes the daily mean by hour. It is a traffic and mixing-layer proxy, not an hourly AQI.
    • Regional background is constant. In real cities, crop-residue burning, festivals and transported dust can dominate for days.

    Suggested demo for the presentation

    1. Pick Same as baseline. The result sits at the baseline AQI of 71, led by PM2.5.
    2. Pick Industrial expansion. NO₂ takes over as the dominant pollutant and CO₂ rises sharply.
    3. Pick Car-centric sprawl and compare its source split and carbon table with the baseline.
    4. Pick Winter stagnation and untick the same-weather option. The city is unchanged, but low wind and a shallow mixing layer alone push the AQI into the Very Poor range.
    5. Finish with Green transit city and Car-light eco city, and compare AQI, carbon and score with the baseline.
